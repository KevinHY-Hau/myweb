📖 Rules of Shogi (将棋)

Goal. Checkmate the enemy king: attack it so that it cannot escape, be blocked or have the attacker captured. Sente (先手, Black ☗) is at the bottom and moves first; Gote (後手, White ☖) is at the top. The pieces are flat wooden tiles that point toward the enemy, so you can tell the sides apart by the direction, not by colour.

The pieces (20 each)

1 king, 1 rook, 1 bishop, 2 golds, 2 silvers, 2 knights, 2 lances and 9 pawns. The board is 9 x 9. Pieces never move through others, except the knight, which jumps.

Moving and capturing

  • On your turn do one of two things: move a piece, or drop a captured piece (below). You cannot pass.
  • To capture, move onto an enemy piece. It is removed from the board and goes to your hand.
  • Capturing is never compulsory, but you must always get your king out of check.

Promotion (成り)

  • The three rows nearest the enemy are the promotion zone. When a piece moves into, within, or out of the zone you may promote it (flip it over). Pawns, lances, knights and silvers become gold-movers; the bishop becomes a horse and the rook a dragon. Golds and kings do not promote.
  • Promotion is optional, except when a piece would otherwise have no move: a pawn or lance on the last row, or a knight on the last two rows must promote.
  • Promotion is permanent, until the piece is captured. A captured piece goes to the captor's hand as its original, unpromoted type.

Drops (打ち)

  • Instead of moving, you can place a piece from your hand on any empty square, facing forward and unpromoted. It can give check or capture on the next move. This is why shogi games rarely end in a lone king.
  • You may not drop a pawn or lance on the last row, or a knight on the last two rows (it would have no move).
  • Nifu (二歩): you may not drop a pawn in a file where you already have an unpromoted pawn. (A promoted pawn, a tokin, does not count.)
  • Uchifuzume (打ち歩詰め): you may not give checkmate by dropping a pawn. (You may give checkmate by moving a pawn, or by dropping any other piece.)

Check and the end of the game

  • A king under attack is in check. You must answer it: move the king, capture the attacker, or block (by moving or dropping a piece in between). You may never make a move that leaves your own king in check.
  • Checkmate wins. If a side has no legal move at all, it loses.
  • Repetition (千日手): if the same position with the same side to move occurs four times, the game is a draw. (This game does not apply the extra rule that the side giving perpetual check loses.)
  • A game lasting 500 moves is called a draw. Not included: the “impasse” (入玉) point-counting rule.

Reading the notation

The last move is shown like ☗７六歩: ☗ = Sente (☖ = Gote), then the file (column, numbered 9 to 1 from Sente's left to right), then the rank (row, 一 to 九 from the top), then the piece. 成 means it promoted, 打 means it was dropped.

How to use this game

  • Tap one of your pieces; its destinations appear (green = move, red = capture). Tap a square to move. If you can promote, a small question appears. To drop, tap a piece in your hand (the row of 7 tiles under the board) and then an empty square.
  • Settings: computer level, which side you play, piece labels (kanji or English letters), and a helper that rings the pieces you can move.
  • Hint shows a good move. Undo takes back your move and the computer's reply.
