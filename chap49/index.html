📖 Rules of Shatranj

The ancestor of chess. Same 8×8 board and the same aim: checkmate the enemy king. But three pieces are different and the game is slower and more about patient attack.

The pieces

  • King (♚) and Rook (♜) and Knight (♞) move as in chess. There is no castling.
  • Ferz (♛ symbol) is the “queen” of the time, but weak: one step diagonally only.
  • Alfil (♝ symbol) is the “elephant”: it leaps exactly two squares diagonally, jumping over anything. So it only ever reaches 8 squares of the same colour.
  • Pawn (♟) steps one square forward (no double step, no en passant) and captures diagonally. At the far end it becomes a Ferz.
  • Set-up: Rook, Knight, Alfil, King, Ferz, Alfil, Knight, Rook. The kings face each other on the d-file.

How you win

  • Checkmate.
  • Stalemate: in the classical rule, the player with no legal move (and not in check) loses. A setting turns it into a draw.
  • Bare king: take all of the enemy’s other pieces and you win, unless the bared player can answer on the very next move by taking your last piece too (then it is a draw).

Draws in this version

  • The same position three times, or 120 half-moves (plies) with no capture and no pawn move.

Playing here

  • Tap a piece and then a square, or drag it. Dots show legal moves. Tap a piece to see what it does.
  • Pass and play lets two people share the screen. The computer is a casual player: Easy makes mistakes, Hard thinks about 1.5 seconds per move.
  • Shatranj rules differed by time and place; this is the common modern reconstruction.

♟ Shatranj came from India and Persia and was the chess of the Islamic world from about the 7th century, long before the modern queen and bishop. Rules follow the common reconstruction (as in xboard and Pychess); the stalemate rule varies by source, so there is a setting for it. Drawings, sounds, rules code and the computer player are my own.
